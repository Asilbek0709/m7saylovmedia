#31 | Media Convergence
- the process by which media content flows across different platforms, media organisations cooperate, and audiences move from one platform to another.
- Henry Jenkins's Convergence Culture: Where Old and New Media Collide was published by New York University Press in 2006. Jenkins links convergence not only to technologies merging into one device but also to content moving across platforms and to cultural change.
- helps analyse how election coverage is distributed in different formats on websites, social media, television and mobile platforms.

#32 | Platformization
- the process by which social, economic and cultural activity becomes increasingly tied to the infrastructure, rules and algorithms of digital platforms.
- platformization is a concept that developed in contemporary media research on digital platforms. Claiming that a particular person coined it or that it appeared in a particular year would require further historical verification.
- used to study the role of social media in distributing, recommending and delivering political information to audiences.

#33 | Multimedia
- the quality of presenting information in several forms, such as text, images, sound, video, animation and interactive elements.
- the modern use of the term multimedia expanded with the development of computer and digital technology. Without a reliable dedicated source on when the term was first used, no exact year is given.
- describes how election news is delivered through text, infographics, video commentary, audio and interactive visualisations.

#34 | Interactivity
- the ability of users or audiences to interact with media content, the way it is presented, or the people who produce it.
- the concept of interactivity is used in different senses in research on computer interfaces, communication theory and digital media. Rather than asserting flatly that it has no single originator or date, it is better to study its development separately in each field.
- helps analyse online polls, comments, Q&A sessions, live broadcasts and mechanisms for collecting audience views.

#35 | Feedback
- a response returned by the person or audience receiving information to the sender or to the system.
- the concept of feedback is used not only in communication but also in cybernetics and control theory. Norbert Wiener's Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine, published in 1948, is one of the important sources in the scholarly development of feedback in control and communication. This does not mean that Wiener coined the term.
- used to assess how audience questions, objections, comments and requests are taken into account in media work.

#36 | Hybrid Media System
- an information system in which traditional and digital media, political actors and audiences operate in interconnection.
- Andrew Chadwick's The Hybrid Media System: Politics and Power was published in 2013. The author analysed the interdependence of the logics, institutions and actors of older and newer media in political communication.
- makes it possible to analyse comprehensively how television, newspapers, websites, social media and political actors influence one another.

#37 | Algorithmic Curation
- the use of computer algorithms to show, rank or recommend information to users.
- algorithmic curation is linked to the development of digital platforms, search engines and recommendation systems. Identifying a single originator or date of first use would require a dedicated historical source.
- important for studying the order in which election news is shown to audiences and how platform algorithms can affect the visibility of information.

#38 | Audience
- the group of people who receive, follow or use a particular media product.
- the concept of the audience developed with forms of communication from theatre and public speaking to the print press, radio, television and digital media. Its interpretation in modern media research has been shaped by several scholarly traditions.
- helps study who receives election information, what information they need and which media they choose.

#39 | Audience Engagement
- a concept describing audiences' attention to media content, their use of it, their reactions and their participation in conversation.
- audience engagement has been defined and measured in different ways in media research. Online it can include comments, shares, reactions, reading time and other metrics. There is no single originator or date that applies in all cases.
- used to study how actively audiences engage with election content.

#40 | Audience Reach
- the number or share of an audience that information or a media product reached over a given period.
- measuring reach developed with audience measurement methods in radio, television, advertising and media research. The exact way it is measured depends on the platform and the research methodology.
- important for assessing how many people election news reached. Reach cannot be equated with the audience having read the material in full or having believed it.

#41 | Theoretical and Methodological Approach
- the system of theoretical foundations, concepts, principles and scientific methods used to explain a research problem.
- the term belongs to general scientific methodology; it is not a specific media theory created by one person.
- determines which theories and methods are used to study media content, audience response and communication effectiveness in election coverage.

#42 | Empirical Research
- research based on observation, experiment, questionnaires, interviews, documents or other data.
- the idea of empirical knowledge goes back to philosophical traditions long before modern science took shape. It cannot be tied to one author or a single date.
- used to collect media content, survey voters and assess media performance on the basis of concrete data.

#43 | Content Analysis
- a method of systematically analysing text, images, audio, video or other communication content according to set rules. It can be quantitative or qualitative.
- Bernard Berelson's Content Analysis in Communication Research was published in 1952. It set out content analysis as a systematic method for communication research. Although Berelson's work was an important milestone, analysing the content of texts predates it.
- used to code and compare the number of stories about candidates, topics, headlines, sources and the way judgements are made.

#44 | Media Monitoring
- the process of tracking, collecting, sorting and analysing information on a given topic over a set period.
- media monitoring grew out of press-clipping and press-review practice and later the monitoring of radio, television and digital information. Naming a single originator or exact year of origin would require a dedicated historical source.
- makes it possible to track regularly how candidates, parties and election topics are covered in the media during an election period.

#45 | Discourse Analysis
- a set of analytical methods that study how meaning, social relations, views and power relations are formed through language and communication.
- discourse analysis took shape in various theoretical schools. For example, Teun A. van Dijk's News as Discourse was published in 1988 and is considered an important source for analysing news texts from a discourse perspective. That is not the year discourse analysis first appeared.
- used to study how candidates are described in political texts, which concepts recur, and how political events are interpreted.

#46 | Comparative Analysis
- a method of comparing two or more objects, situations, sources or periods against set criteria.
- comparison is one of the general methods of scientific inquiry, not a distinct media theory created by any one person.
- makes it possible to compare the information content of state and non-state media, different media platforms or different election campaigns.

#47 | Observation Method
- a method of watching and recording the characteristics and changes of a research object according to a predetermined procedure.
- observation is one of the oldest methods of scientific research; it has no single originator or date of origin.
- used to record systematically media activity during elections, press conferences, journalistic events or conversations on digital platforms.

#48 | Survey
- a method of collecting people's views, experiences, behaviour or information through standardised questions.
- mass polling and methods of measuring voter opinion developed widely in the 20th century. Gallup's historical materials identify the 1930s as an important period in the development of modern public opinion polling in the United States. This does not mean that Gallup invented the survey method.
- used to study voters' information sources, trust in the media and awareness of the election.

#49 | Source Diversity
- drawing on different people, organisations, documents and points of view in news content.
- the term is linked to the principles of working with sources in journalism, information quality and media pluralism. No single originator or date of origin is established.
- important for determining whether election news relies on a single political actor or official source, or covers a range of views.

#50 | Balanced Reporting
- the effort to present the relevant important viewpoints and parties to a story fairly and in context.
- balanced reporting is a normative principle linked to journalistic ethics and professional standards. No single originator or exact date of origin can be given.
- important when covering different candidates, setting out their positions and checking political claims. Balance does not automatically mean giving each side equal time; context and evidence must also be taken into account.

#51 | Information Quality
- the set of properties of information such as relevance, accuracy, completeness, timeliness, clarity and reliability.
- assessing information quality developed through different criteria in research on information systems, records management, journalism and management. There is no single author or date for all fields.
- used to assess how correct, complete, current and useful election news is for the audience.

#52 | Information Credibility
- the grounds for trusting information or its source, including the source's reputation, evidence, verifiability and the consistency of the data.
- credibility has been studied in several research traditions in communication, psychology, journalism and information science. It has no single originator or year of origin.
- used to assess whether news about candidates and the electoral process relies on trustworthy sources, checks facts and avoids false information.

#53 | Information Accuracy
- the correspondence of the facts, names, dates, figures and other details in a story to reality.
- accuracy is one of the basic requirements of fact-checking and professional journalistic standards. It is not a theory created by any particular scholar.
- important when checking that the election date, voting procedure, information about candidates and results are reported without errors.

#54 | Timeliness
- preparing information and delivering it to the audience at the right time relative to the event or process.
- timeliness is a professional criterion in journalism linked to the currency of information and the news production process. It cannot be tied to one author or date.
- used to assess whether important changes in the electoral process, official decisions and results are reported without delay. Timeliness does not, however, remove the requirement to check facts.

#55 | Communication Effectiveness
- a concept expressing how far the result of communication has achieved its set goals. Effectiveness can be measured by whether a message reached the audience, was understood, was remembered or influenced the intended action.
- the concept is used in different ways in communication theory, organisational management and evaluation research. Naming a single originator or exact year of origin would require a dedicated source.
- helps assess whether election content reached its target audience, was clear, and how well it met information needs.

#56 | Institutional Legitimacy
- the acceptance by society of an institution's or authority's powers and activities as lawful, acceptable or justified.
- Max Weber's work on authority and legitimacy holds an important place in the development of this concept in sociology. Institutional legitimacy was later studied widely in organisation theory as well. When choosing a specific work and year of publication, Weber's various works and their publication history need to be distinguished.
- used to analyse media content on how the public accepts election institutions, electoral rules and results. Legitimacy cannot be measured through media coverage alone.

#57 | The MS–7: Saylov–Media Model
- a model for assessing media coverage of electoral processes against several criteria.
- the MS–7 model was developed by the University of Journalism and Mass Communications of Uzbekistan and Jamshid Namozov. On 30 July 2026, the Intellectual Property Center under the Ministry of Justice of the Republic of Uzbekistan issued certificate of deposit No. 010589.
- the system is designed for the comprehensive assessment, monitoring and comparative analysis (ranking) of how digital mass media cover electoral processes, based on a single scientific and innovative approach.

#58 | SMSI
- SMSI is a composite index expressing how effectively the media cover an election.
- SMSI (Saylov Media Samaradorligi Indexi, the Election Media Effectiveness Index) was developed to express assessment results as a single numerical indicator. It makes it possible to measure numerically how effectively digital media cover electoral processes, compare platforms, build rankings and track development over time.
- a comprehensive methodology has been developed for assessing how effectively digital mass media cover electoral processes.

#59 | Comprehensive Assessment
- a method of assessing an object or process not by a single indicator but by several interrelated criteria and dimensions.
- comprehensive assessment is a general methodological approach used in many fields of research.
- makes it possible to analyse media content not only by volume or reach but also by quality of content, source diversity, accuracy and other criteria.

#60 | Composite Indicator
- an indicator that expresses the overall state of a complex phenomenon or object by combining several separate indicators in a set way.
- integral or composite indicators are used in many fields. One of the international methodological sources on them is the Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators: Methodology and User Guide (2008), prepared by the OECD and the European Commission's Joint Research Centre. It describes stages such as selecting indicators, normalisation, weighting and aggregation.
